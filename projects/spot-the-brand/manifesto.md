# Spot the Brand · Manifesto

The thing we believe, before anything else.

---

## I.

**Brands live in the wild.**

Not in hashtags. Not in @mentions. Not in the curated, filtered, search-indexed sliver of social media that legacy tools learned to read in 2015.

Brands live in someone's hand at a festival. On a shelf in the background of a kitchen video. In a get-ready-with-me where the product is the punchline but never named. In a story that disappears in 24 hours but reaches 40.000 people. In a TikTok where the can flashes for half a second and four hundred kids drink the same one next weekend.

That's where brands actually live. And that's what brand monitoring has been blind to.

## II.

**We see images. They read text.**

Every brand monitoring tool of the last decade was built around words. Hashtags. Captions. Comments. They parse what people *wrote* about your brand.

But people don't write about brands anymore. They show them.

The shift from text-first to visual-first social happened years ago. Brand monitoring didn't catch up. So a generation of marketers has been told their brand reach is the 14% with a caption mention, when the other 86% is happening in front of them in pixels.

Spot the Brand is what brand monitoring becomes when it can see.

## III.

**Computer vision finally works for this.**

The technology that made us possible only matured in 2024-2025. Gemini Flash dropped the cost of high-quality vision inference by 5× in 12 months. Apify and adjacent infra made legitimate, scalable social scraping a commodity. A single brand can now be visually monitored across IG + TikTok for less than what one Storyclash seat costs.

We didn't invent the technique. We're the first to apply it to brand monitoring as a category. The window for category-defining work is open. We're moving into it.

## IV.

**Earned media is the most underpriced asset in marketing.**

A brand manager spends €50.000 paying an influencer to post about their product. That same brand manager doesn't know that, last week, four creators with smaller followings posted organically — drinking, holding, displaying the same product — and reached 200.000 viewers between them.

Those four creators are the brand's actual audience. They opted in. They chose the product. They post for free, with conviction.

A brand that knows who those people are can amplify them, partner with them, send them product, build something real. A brand that doesn't know them is just buying ads.

Our job is to make sure no brand has to operate without that knowledge ever again.

## V.

**Anti-creep.**

We do not track people. We track products in publicly posted content. We do not build profiles of individuals beyond what's required to surface the brand-relevant moments. We do not store facial recognition data. We do not enable surveillance of consumers.

If a brand wants to enter the relationship with the creators we surface, that's their choice and the creator's choice. We are the discovery layer. The relationship is theirs.

This matters. The technology that makes our product possible can be used badly. We refuse those use cases up front.

## VI.

**We're built for challengers.**

The brands that need us most are the ones too small to hire an army of social-listening interns. The Dutch hard seltzer brand competing against four multinationals. The craft beer that grew from a garage. The DTC personal-care label that just hit profitability. The challenger who can't afford to be invisible to half their own audience.

The big brands will adopt us eventually. We'll help them. But our compass is the challenger — the team of three to thirty people who need to know what's happening to their brand, and have neither the time nor the budget for the legacy approach.

## VII.

**Visible to everyone who matters.**

We make the dashboard. The brand pays €500 or €1.500 a month. The marketing team finds creators they didn't know about. The creators get noticed and amplified. The product gets bought by people who first saw it on someone they already follow.

Nobody loses in this system. That's the bet.
