# Spot the Brand · Sales Playbook v0.2

How we sell the brand, not a single customer.

> Read [`manifesto.md`](manifesto.md) and [`brandbook.md`](brandbook.md) first. Sales conversations flow from those.

## 1. The thesis we sell

We don't sell features. We sell a category shift.

The category shift, in one sentence:

> Brand monitoring built around text is blind to roughly 80% of how brands actually show up in social today. Visual detection finally works. Spot the Brand is the first product applying it.

Every conversation either gets the prospect to nod at that sentence, or it doesn't. If they nod, the rest is logistics. If they don't, no demo will save us.

So: never open with the product. Open with the insight. Sell the new world, then sell the tool.

## 2. The 30-second pitch (memorize)

> Most brand monitoring tools read text. Hashtags. Mentions. Captions. But people don't write about brands anymore, they show them. Eighty percent of the visual mentions of your brand don't have a hashtag, and your current tool can't see them. We use computer vision to find your product in the actual image. Daily. Automatically. €500 a month.

Land on the price. Wait. Most prospects respond to the gap between what their tools cost and what we cost. That's your opening.

## 3. ICP — who we sell to

Three tiers, ranked by how fast we can close.

### Tier A — Closeable in 2-3 calls

European challenger brands with:
- Visually distinctive product (a can, a bottle, a wrapper, a tube, a uniform — something that reads as the brand without text)
- Active social presence (≥3 posts/week IG or TikTok)
- Marketing team of 3-30 people
- €1M–€50M revenue
- Has used Storyclash, Brand24, Tagger, Modash, or built internal social-listening — and felt the gap

Concrete examples (research these; reach out one per week):
- Hard seltzer / RTD: Klar, Captain Morgan Spritz NL, hard-seltzer challengers entering NL
- Craft beer: Vandestreek, Lowlander, Brouwerij 't IJ, Charlie's Beer
- Functional drinks: RAW Superdrink, Crisp, Mother
- DTC personal care: about-time, Mother Mary, Beebop
- Specialty food/snacks: Tony's Chocolonely, Stroopwafels by Lotte, Cocoa Pods
- Spirits / cocktail-in-can: Klar, Hyke Gin, RTD cocktail brands

### Tier B — Closeable in 3-6 calls (longer cycle, bigger ACV)

Larger established brands with a frustrated brand manager:
- Heineken NL or Bavaria (specific RTD or specialty lines, not flagship)
- Vrumona (Crystal Clear, Sourcy with brand insights)
- Lipton NL via PepsiCo
- Rituals (multi-product personal care; high visual brand recognition)
- Tony's Chocolonely (across product variants)
- HEMA private-label SKU lines

Approach: warm intro via existing JackandAI / D&L network.

### Tier C — 6-12 month enterprise cycle

Multinationals: Coca-Cola NL, Unilever NL, Mars Wrigley. Sell via custom enterprise contracts, not Stripe self-serve. Don't lead with these — they buy slower than our runway demands.

## 5. Pricing anchors

| Plan | Monthly | Best for |
|------|---------|----------|
| Starter | €500 | 1 brand, weekly insight report, monthly scan cadence |
| Pro | €1.500 | 1 brand, daily scans, Slack + email alerts, Pro verify, team seats |
| Enterprise | Custom | Multi-brand, white-label, API, SLA, dedicated CSM |

### Anchoring lines that work

- "Storyclash is ~€1.000-2.500/month and only catches what's hashtagged. We see what they miss. Starter is €500."
- "The cost of missing one tier-1 creator moment is higher than 6 months of our Pro plan."
- "If our tool saves your team four hours of manual scrolling a week, the math closes at Starter alone."

### Pricing don'ts

- Don't lead with price. Lead with the gap. Price closes the deal, doesn't open it.
- Don't discount in the first conversation. If they push back, offer trial first.
- Don't bundle Enterprise features into Starter as a "favor". That's how SaaS pricing falls apart.

Open at `https://spotyourbrand.com/?demo=1` (this is our STELZ-as-reference demo URL).

| 6:00-8:00 | Show creator profile page + tiering | "Every creator gets scored 0-10 by an AI for relevance. Tier-1 creators get auto-alerted to your Slack the moment they post." |
| 8:00-10:00 | Show weekly PDF report | "This goes to the brand manager's inbox Monday morning. Three pages. Top creators, top hits, trend vs last week. Ready to share." |
| 10:00-12:00 | Open account.html — pricing, team, alerts settings | "Pro tier, daily scans, Slack alerts, weekly PDF, team seats — €1.500/month. Starter is €500 if you want monthly cadence." |
| 12:00-14:00 | Walk through 14-day trial setup | "Trial takes 24 hours to provision. First hits in 48 hours. No CC required. If it doesn't earn its keep in two weeks, you walk away clean." |
| 14:00-15:00 | Close | "What would make this a yes for you? If it's a trial, I can have you live by Tuesday. If it's more proof, I can run a 90-second mini-scan on your brand right now." |

Two things the demo script does on purpose:
1. **The customer (STELZ) appears at minute 1 and exits at minute 4.** They are proof, not protagonist.
2. **The product gets the most stage time, not the customer's data.** Prospects need to picture themselves using this.

## 7. Customer-story discipline (this is the new rule)

Spot the Brand is a brand. STELZ is a customer. Treat the difference like load-bearing wall.

### When STELZ should appear in a sales conversation
- As proof a real production deployment exists
- As proof the magnitude of the gap (80%+ blind spot) is real and measurable
- As proof the technology works at scale (436 hits, 545 creators, daily for 90 days)

### When STELZ should NOT appear
- As the implied use case (the prospect needs to see their own brand here, not STELZ's)
- As the entire conversation (we're not a STELZ-monitoring tool)
- As shorthand for "the product"

### Language patterns

Bad: "Look at STELZ's dashboard, this is what you get."
Good: "Spot the Brand works like this. Here, as one example, is what it looks like for STELZ in production."

Bad: "STELZ found 436 mentions in 90 days."
Good: "Across the brands we've measured, the average visual-only mention rate is 80%. For one customer that meant 436 mentions a quarter."

Bad: "STELZ uses our Pro tier."
Good: "Pro tier is what most early customers pick — daily scans, alerts, weekly PDF."

## 8. Objection counters

**"We already use Storyclash."**
> "Good. How many of your mentions come without a hashtag or @mention? Most brands we audit are at 80% or more. Our trial is two weeks, no CC. Run us in parallel for two weeks; if we don't find substantially more, we go away."

**"This is too expensive."**
> "Starter is €500/month. About what it costs to commission one piece of agency creative. Versus the cost of missing the creators who are organically your audience."

**"Can we trust the AI?"**
> "Every detection above 0.7 confidence gets re-checked by a more expensive model — under 5% false positive rate in production. Borderline cases get re-verified at higher resolution by a second model. Every detection is reviewable in the dashboard. You can mark any false positive in one click and the system learns."

**"What about GDPR / scraping?"**
> "We monitor publicly posted content only. We don't store facial data. We don't profile individuals beyond what's needed to attribute a brand mention. We have a DPA, a privacy policy, and we'll sign whatever your legal team needs."

**"Can we get a free trial?"**
> "Yes. 14 days, Pro features, no credit card. Provisioned within 24 hours. First hits in 48 hours. If it doesn't earn its keep, you walk away."

**"How does this compare to Tagger / Modash?"**
> "They're great for outbound — you've got a list of creators, you want to manage them. We're inbound — finding creators you don't yet know who are already engaged. Different jobs. They tend to coexist."

**"You're a new company. What if you go away?"**
> "Fair. We're early. Reality: the underlying tech is commodity (Apify, Gemini, Supabase). If we disappeared tomorrow, you'd lose the dashboard and the IP, but the workflow is replicable. We mitigate this with a data export feature — you can pull everything out as CSV any day."

**"Why don't we just hire an intern to do this manually?"**
> "An intern scrolling Instagram three hours a day will catch about 5% of what we surface. Confidence interval: based on the manual baseline we measured before automation. The math doesn't work."

**"Can we trust the data privacy?"**
> "We monitor publicly posted content. We do not store facial recognition data. We do not enable consumer surveillance — we surface posts that already exist publicly. SOC2 prep is on our roadmap. If you need a specific compliance commitment, let's discuss."

- [ ] Brand has consumer-facing social presence

## 11. Trial → paid conversion plays

The trial is 14 days. Most prospects pick day 13 to decide. Engineer those 14 days.

| Day | Touchpoint |
|-----|------------|
| 0 | Welcome email (auto). First hits queue. |
| 2 | Manual check-in: "First 24h of hits in, anything surprising?" |
| 5 | Weekly highlight report (auto, beautiful PDF) |
| 7 | Half-time call: 15 min, walk through what's been found, identify a tier-1 creator to act on |
| 10 | "Action moment" email: pick one creator they should DM today. Make it concrete. |
| 13 | Trial-end email + Stripe checkout link |
| 14 | Trial ends. If no decision, manual ping. If still no: archive, retarget in 3 months. |

## 15. What "good" looks like by month

- Month 1: 25 cold + 10 warm conversations. 5 demos. 2 trials. 0-1 paid.
- Month 2: pipeline maturing. 5-8 demos. 3-5 trials. 1-2 paid.
- Month 3: first STELZ-class second logo. 8-12 demos. 4-6 trials. 2-3 paid.
- Month 6: target 10 paying brands. €10-15k MRR.
- Month 12: target 30 paying brands. €40-60k MRR.

Anchor your week to "did I move 5 prospects this week", not "did I close one this week".

## 16. The one thing that determines whether this works

How well the first 5 conversations go. Lukas + Meinte should both be in every one of the first 5 demo calls. After 5, debrief and decide what to keep, what to drop. Then scale.
