# Spot the Brand · Customer examples library

How to talk about real customers in sales conversations + content without making them the protagonist.

> The product brand is **Spot the Brand**. Customers prove the brand works. They are evidence, never identity. Read [`brandbook.md`](brandbook.md) §4 if this is unclear.

## 1. The structure for every example

A customer example always has the same shape:

> **Spot the Brand** [does the thing]. **One example**: [customer/category] [achieved this outcome]. **The pattern** [applies to your category].

| Layer | Role |
|-------|------|
| Brand-first claim | Establishes what Spot the Brand does |
| Customer evidence | Proves the claim with a real number |
| Pattern translation | Bridges to the prospect's reality |

If you can't write all three layers, the example isn't ready to use.

## 2. The current public example: a Dutch hard seltzer brand (anonymized in copy)

### What we can say

| Fact | Use |
|------|-----|
| 90 days in production | Use as proof of stability |
| 436 verified product hits | Use as scale anchor |
| 545 creators tracked | Use as creator-discovery anchor |
| 86% of hits had no #hashtag | Use as the central category claim |
| <5% false positive rate | Use as accuracy anchor |
| 0.95 avg confidence | Use only with technical audiences |

### How to say it (good)

> "Spot the Brand finds brand mentions that legacy tools miss. A Dutch hard seltzer brand we work with ran us for 90 days and found 436 visual mentions — 86% of which had no hashtag. The pattern (most mentions visible-but-untagged) holds across categories."

### How NOT to say it (bad)

> ~~"STELZ is our customer. Look, here's their dashboard. This is what you get."~~

Two problems with the bad version: (1) it makes a commercial claim that isn't yet true, and (2) it frames a single customer as the protagonist instead of the product. Stay descriptive about the data, neutral about the relationship.

## 3. Anonymization protocol

When the customer hasn't explicitly green-lit being named in a specific context, we anonymize. Default to anonymized.

### Levels of disclosure

| Level | When to use | How |
|-------|-------------|-----|
| Named | A customer has signed off in writing on being named in marketing | "[Brand], the Netherlands' #1 [category], runs Spot the Brand…" |
| **Categorical** (default) | Real data, real customer, but no explicit Named opt-in | "A Dutch hard seltzer brand we work with…" |
| Sector | When category alone is enough proof | "A challenger F&B brand in the Netherlands…" |
| Hypothetical | Pre-sale, illustrative for a brand we don't yet work with | "Imagine your brand on this dashboard…" |

**Current status**: STELZ is on the platform in a design-partner / pilot capacity. Not a paying customer yet. Default to **Categorical** when referencing in marketing — describe the data and results, don't claim a commercial relationship. The dashboard URL `?brand=stelz` is fine because the data is real and they've greenlit public-demo visibility; what we don't do is say "STELZ is our customer" in copy or pitches.

### Visual anonymization

When showing screenshots in marketing content where a customer name appears:
- Blur or solid-block customer logos
- Replace creator handles with `@redacted-1` style placeholders
- Bounding boxes can stay (they're our brand mark)
- Captions: replace with the bracket `[redacted]` where customer names appear

### Creator anonymization

When showing creator content in our marketing:
- Show the bounding-box-around-product, not the creator's face
- Don't disclose follower count + handle together unless they've given permission
- Don't quote creator captions verbatim without permission
- If in doubt, ask the customer (they have the relationship with their creators)

## 4. Pre-launch examples (illustrative, not real)

We don't yet have 5 reference customers. For pitches and ads while we're building the customer base, we use **illustrative** examples that make the brand promise concrete without claiming we already delivered it.

### Pattern: "Imagine this for your brand"

> "Imagine [BRAND]'s dashboard on Spot the Brand. Last week:
> - 47 new creator hits across IG + TikTok
> - 3 tier-1 creators identified you didn't know about
> - 1 brand partnership opportunity surfaced and a follow-up DM drafted
>
> This is what one paying customer's first 30 days looks like. Yours will look different — but the pattern holds."

This is honest. It says "imagine" not "we found". Use it freely in cold-outreach decks.

### Pattern: "What we found for a comparable brand"

> "Spot the Brand works best for brands with a visually distinctive product. The Dutch hard seltzer customer we ran for 90 days has a similar profile to [BRAND]: distinctive can, NL-first social presence, challenger market position. Their numbers were 436 visual mentions per quarter. We'd expect a comparable order of magnitude for [BRAND]."

This bridges the proof to the prospect without overclaiming. The math is honest: similar brand profile suggests similar numbers, not guaranteed.

## 5. The "library of moments" approach

For social campaigns and pitches, build a library of **anonymized detection moments** — small, atomic stories that prove the brand without anchoring on one customer.

### Example moments (build these out as we collect them)

| ID | Category | Anonymized fact | When to use |
|----|----------|-----------------|-------------|
| M-001 | Hard seltzer | "Found a tier-1 creator drinking the brand at a music festival. Zero hashtags. 22k followers. Brand had never heard of her." | Discovery proof, festival/lifestyle context |
| M-002 | F&B challenger | "Detected a city-wide vending placement (50+ posts, 4 days, 0 brand activation budget)." | Earned-media proof |
| M-003 | DTC | "Identified an organic micro-creator network: 12 connected creators all using the product, none paid." | Creator-graph proof |

Add a new moment every week, anonymized. Use as story fodder for content, sales, and case studies.

## 6. Customer story templates

For when we have explicit permission to name a customer:

### Template A: "A customer story" (one-pager)

```markdown
# How [BRAND] uses Spot the Brand

[BRAND] is a [one-line description].

**The problem**: [What they were missing before Spot the Brand]

**What changed**: [The metric or capability that improved]

**One specific moment**: [A concrete example, ideally with a real outcome]

**The math**: [Before vs after numbers, conservatively stated]

**What they say**: [One quote, ideally]

Try Spot the Brand for your brand: spotyourbrand.com
```

### Template B: 60-second video story

```
[0-5s]   Logo + customer category, no name yet ("a Dutch hard seltzer brand...")
[5-15s]  The problem ("their old tool tracked 14% of mentions")
[15-30s] The product change (1-2 dashboard screens)
[30-45s] The outcome ("436 mentions found, 9 new tier-1 creators")
[45-60s] Reveal of brand name + customer quote (with permission)
```

The reveal at the end works because the audience has been investing attention in the brand premise first. The customer is the payoff, not the lead.

## 7. The discipline rules

These keep us aligned:

1. **The first sentence of any sales/marketing/PR statement is about Spot the Brand, not about a customer.**
2. **The customer name appears at minute 1 of a demo and exits by minute 4.**
3. **Sales decks have customers on slide 6, not slide 2.**
4. **Social posts that mention a customer also explicitly mention Spot the Brand as the actor doing the work.**
5. **We never say "this is what you get" while pointing at a customer's dashboard.** We say "this is one example of what the product surfaces".
6. **When the customer cancels (which will happen eventually for some), the brand survives unchanged.** That's the test.

## 8. Internal review process

Before publishing any externally-visible customer reference:

- [ ] Have we asked the customer about this specific use? (For STELZ: existing relationship covers most public-demo and marketing references, but escalate non-obvious uses.)
- [ ] Could a competitor read this and learn something proprietary about the customer's marketing strategy? (If yes, soften.)
- [ ] Does the post/slide/email open with the brand, or open with the customer? (Must open with brand.)
- [ ] Is the pattern translation present (the "your reality will be different but the pattern applies" line)?
- [ ] Are numbers conservative? (Always under-promise.)

If any answer is "no", revise.
